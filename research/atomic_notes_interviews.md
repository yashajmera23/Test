# Atomic Notes — User Interviews

Source: board image titled "Atomic Notes" (uploaded mid-session). Two columns by participant. Colour: yellow = P1, pink = P2.

## Atomic Notes: Ritika (P1) — yellow stickies

- Lives with a flatmate in Mahadevapura, Bangalore
- No dedicated cook; they manage the kitchen themselves
- Both are busy working professionals
- Doesn't cook often; it's occasional, not daily
- Breakfast is usually a protein shake (no cooking needed)
- Lunch is mostly ordered from outside or the office canteen
- Uses a notes app informally to track needed items
- Does not use a dedicated grocery tracking app
- Uses multiple delivery platforms (Zepto, Amazon Now, Blinkit) — not loyal to one
- Switches platforms based on price comparison
- No single app can track her full purchase pattern because of cross-platform ordering
- Shifted from physical stores (Dmart/LuLu Mall) to online for convenience at similar cost
- Stockouts are minor inconveniences because "everything is available"
- Reaction to stockout: immediately order on a quick-commerce app; no panic
- Fast delivery (10 minutes) removes urgency and stress
- Stockouts often noticed mid-cooking when a specific ingredient is missing
- Uses physical sticky notes on the fridge sometimes as a reminder
- A digital reminder would only work if she ordered from one platform consistently
- Finds "frequently ordered" suggestions mildly useful; doesn't feel need for an extra feature
- Open to reminders but admits she might "ignore it once" because urgency is low
- Does not feel a strong need for a dedicated reminder feature; existing apps loosely cover the need

## Atomic Notes: Interviewee 2 (P2) — pink stickies

- Lives in a PG in Mumbai with two flatmates
- Has a cook who comes twice daily (breakfast/lunch morning, dinner evening)
- Cook is primary kitchen manager; coordinates grocery needs specifically with her
- Working professional, hybrid schedule, finishes work by 7–8 PM
- Masalas and pulses stored in boxes she never sees — no visibility on stock
- Fridge items easier to notice (visible daily)
- Cook tells her 1–2 days before something runs out; she often forgets to order even after being told
- Cook has to remind her again at night when item is completely gone and can't cook
- Last-minute pressure: cook says "no onions, how do I cook?" → stressed ordering
- Sometimes cook waits ~10 minutes while they order, blocking cook's work
- Does not actively monitor pantry stock ("we never check")
- Informally appointed herself "grocery manager" for the flatmates
- Low-value items (₹30–40) skipped because they don't meet free delivery threshold
- Items only ordered when cart reaches free delivery minimum; otherwise deferred and forgotten
- Example: Needed AAA battery since morning, still hadn't ordered because nothing else in cart
- Tried sticky notes on fridge — failed because flatmates had different habits
- Keeps mental notes but forgets by the time she sits down to order
- Tried using app cart as wishlist; clears it for urgent orders and loses planned items
- Wants a "separate cart" for planned vs urgent orders (doesn't exist today)
- Stockouts frequent — at least twice in the last 15 days
- Already overwhelmed by notifications; fears another one won't get through
- Open to ideas but unsure she would follow through
- Would only act on reminders if they bundled multiple items to cross ₹150 free delivery threshold
- Notification for a single low-value item (e.g. ₹30) is useless — can't act on it alone
- Persistent notifications (that aren't cleared) might work better until action is taken
- Vegetables: 3 days notice too long — short shelf life, would go bad
- Bhindi / short-shelf-life veg: wouldn't act on warning; would cook something else
- Masalas/pulses: weekly warning would work — bought every 2–3 months
- Essential staples (dal/chawal/atta): would act on warning — running out is a "big issue"
- Clear mental split: essential staples vs short-shelf-life vs rare masalas/pulses
