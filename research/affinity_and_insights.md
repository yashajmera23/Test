# COL 3 — Affinity Groups & COL 4 — Insight Labels

Source: board image uploaded mid-session (Affinity Groups + Insight Labels).

## Affinity Groups

### 1. Kitchen Ownership & Awareness
- P1: Manages kitchen herself — no cook.
- P2: Cook manages kitchen — she has almost zero visibility.
- P1: Notices stockout mid-cooking (self-discovered).
- P2: Masalas/pulses in boxes — completely invisible until cook flags.
- P2: Fridge items easier — at least visible daily.
- Both: Neither actively checks pantry stock proactively.

### 2. Stockout Trigger Moment
- P1: Finds out mid-cooking — "we were about to cook mixed veg, no capsicum."
- P2: Cook told her 1-2 days early — but she forgot to order anyway.
- P2: Cook waited until night — "pyaaz nahi hai, khana kaise banau" (no onions, how do I cook).
- P2: Cook's work blocked while waiting for delivery — 10 min wait.
- P2: Stockouts happen 2x in last 15 days — not a rare event.
- P2: Stockout feels frustrating — cook dependency creates pressure.
- P1: Stockout feels minor — fast delivery = low urgency.

### 3. Workarounds Tried (and Failed)
- P1: Sticky notes on fridge — used sometimes, not consistent.
- P2: Cart-as-wishlist — cleared during urgent orders, items lost.
- P1: Notes app for informal tracking — no system.
- P2: Wanted separate cart for planned vs urgent — feature doesn't exist.
- P2: Sticky notes on fridge — failed, flatmates didn't maintain.
- Both: Workaround attempted = real pain signal. No workaround = low pain (P1).

### 4. Cart Value & Ordering Behavior
- P2: Low-value items (₹30-40) not ordered alone — below free delivery min.
- P1: Cross-platform ordering breaks any single app's pattern recognition.
- P2: Orders only when cart value hits ₹150 free delivery threshold.
- P2: When forced (cook waiting), adds extra items like cucumber to meet threshold.
- P2: AAA battery needed since morning — still unordered, nothing to add to cart.
- Both: Cart value threshold is a real friction point in reorder behavior.

### 5. Attitude Toward Reminders / Alerts
- P1: Open but would 'ignore it once' — low urgency user.
- P2: Alert only useful if bundles items to cross ₹150 threshold.
- P2: Already overwhelmed by app notifications — skeptical.
- P2: Persistent notification (not cleared) more effective than one-time ping.
- P2: Single low-value item alert = useless, can't act on ₹30 pyaaz alone.
- P1: Reminder useful only if she orders from one platform — cross-platform breaks it.

### 6. Lead Time Needed (Core Research Q)
- P2: Vegetables — 3 days too long, shelf life too short, would go bad.
- P2: Bhindi/perishables — wouldn't act, would just cook something else.
- P2: Dal/chawal/atta — essential, would act on a weekly warning.
- P1: Delivery is 10 min — even same-day alert feels sufficient.
- P2: Masalas/pulses — bought every 2-3 months, even weekly alert is fine.
- KEY: Lead time is category-dependent, not person-dependent.

## Insight Labels

### INSIGHT 1: Two Distinct User Types
P1 (no cook, self-managing) vs P2 (cook-dependent, low kitchen visibility). Different problem shapes — P1's pain is mild/optional, P2's pain is structural and frequent. The target segment is P2.

### INSIGHT 2: The Cook is the Real Information System
For P2, the cook IS the pantry tracker. The problem isn't 'no system' — it's that the cook tells her too late (when item is fully gone) and she forgets to act even when told early. The feature needs to replace the 'forget-to-act' gap, not just replicate what the cook does.

### INSIGHT 3: Cart Value Threshold Blocks Reordering
A single low-value item (₹30 pyaaz) cannot trigger a reorder alone — free delivery minimum (₹150) creates a systemic blocker. Reminder features must either bundle items together or help users hit the threshold — not just notify about one item.

### INSIGHT 4: Lead Time is Category-Dependent
No single buffer works for all grocery types:
- Vegetables -> same-day or next-day
- Staples (dal, rice, atta) -> 3-7 days
- Masalas/pulses -> weekly or even monthly
The feature's reminder logic needs category-aware lead times, not a universal buffer.

### INSIGHT 5: Workaround Intensity = Pain Intensity
P2 tried sticky notes, cart-as-wishlist, and even wanted a 'separate cart' feature. P1 tried sticky notes too but gave up quickly — her pain was low so no strong workaround emerged. The effort invested in workarounds is a direct proxy for how real the problem is.

### INSIGHT 6: Notification Fatigue is Real
P2 already gets too many notifications from Instamart and other apps. A generic reminder will be ignored. For a notification to work it must: (a) be persistent until acted on, (b) bundle enough items to make ordering worthwhile, (c) feel rare/important — not one of many daily pings.
